# TrialDiff v0.2.1 Diagnostic Plan

Status: diagnostic scope
Date: 2026-06-18

TrialDiff v0.2 failed its severity-calibration gate. v0.2.1 is not a rule-tuning sprint yet. It is a diagnostic pass that separates robust classifier failures from reviewer disagreement and rubric-boundary problems before any classifier rule is changed.

The purpose is to keep the failed calibration useful as evidence rather than turning the same 91 reviewed records into a tuning target.

## 1. Starting Point

The v0.2 calibration report is frozen in `SEVERITY_CALIBRATION_v0.2.md`.

The v0.2 result:

- Reviewer 2: critical downgrade rate 90.0%; critical+high downgrade rate 68.3%.

The buyer-facing brief remains blocked.

## 2. Confirmed Findings

These findings are sufficiently robust to guide v0.2.1 work:

1. The critical tier over-fires badly.
   - Both reviewers rejected most TrialDiff-critical records as critical.
   - This is not a marginal threshold issue.

2. `timeline_significant_shift` over-fires as high.
   - Reviewer 1 reviewed 12 of 16 sampled `timeline_significant_shift` critical/high records as medium or low.
   - Reviewer 2 reviewed all 16 as medium or low.

3. Results-reconciliation-like outcome edits need explicit suppression.
   - Outcome text edits co-incident with results-section population or `hasResults` transition were commonly down-weighted.

4. Terminal-status records with present but business-like `whyStopped` text are generally high, not critical.
   - Missing or placeholder-only `whyStopped` remains a separate higher-priority condition.

These are candidate classifier-rule problems. They do not require more evidence before being treated as likely v0.2.1 rule targets.

## 3. Open Boundary Questions

These findings are not yet clean rule failures:

1. High vs. medium boundary.
   - Reviewer 1 and Reviewer 2 diverged strongly on how far to downgrade many TrialDiff critical/high records.
   - This may indicate classifier over-firing, but it may also indicate that the rubric's high/medium boundary is underspecified.

2. Very large timeline slips during active recruitment.
   - Some large timeline movements occurred outside late-stage or post-completion contexts.
   - The diagnostic must decide whether magnitude alone can make such records high priority, or whether late-stage timing remains required.

3. Secondary-outcome additions before completion.
   - The diagnostic must distinguish routine exploratory/correlative additions from changes that materially affect interpretability.

No high-tier classifier rule should be revised solely from these boundary areas until the disagreement analysis below is complete.

## 4. Reviewer-Disagreement Analysis

The diagnostic must classify each R1/R2 disagreement record before any rule edit.

Minimum set:

- all records where Reviewer 1 and Reviewer 2 assigned different priority tiers;
- all records where TrialDiff assigned `critical` or `high` and at least one reviewer assigned `medium` or `low`;
- all records where Reviewer 1 assigned `high` and Reviewer 2 assigned `medium` or `low`;
- all records where Reviewer 2 assigned `high` and Reviewer 1 assigned `medium` or `low`.

Each disagreement must receive exactly one primary diagnostic label:

- `rubric_gap`: the rubric genuinely does not specify the relevant high/medium or critical/high boundary clearly enough.
- `judgment_variance`: the rubric is reasonably clear, but the record sits near an irreducibly judgment-sensitive boundary.
- `reviewer_error`: one reviewer appears to have misapplied a clear rubric or missed a material visible fact.

Optional secondary labels may include:

- `results_reconciliation`
- `timeline_magnitude`
- `late_stage_timing`
- `primary_outcome_substance`
- `secondary_outcome_substance`
- `terminal_status_explanation`
- `enrollment_delta`
- `intervention_or_arm_change`
- `large_patch_noise`

Required output:

`V0.2.1_DISAGREEMENT_ANALYSIS.md`

The analysis must include a table with:

- `review_record_id`
- TrialDiff severity
- TrialDiff category
- Reviewer 1 priority
- Reviewer 2 priority
- primary diagnostic label
- secondary label if useful
- short rationale
- action implied

The action implied must be one of:

- revise rubric;
- revise classifier rule;
- accept reviewer variance;
- annotate reviewer error;
- defer pending expanded corpus.

## 5. Rubric Revision Independence

If the diagnostic recommends sharpening the rubric, the revision must be done from review-priority principle, not from its effect on classifier agreement.

The revised rubric must not be tuned to make TrialDiff pass the v0.2 sample.

Process:

1. Identify rubric gaps from blinded reviewer disagreements.
2. Rewrite the high/medium or critical/high boundary in plain review-priority terms.
3. Freeze the revised rubric in git before any re-scoring or re-calibration.
4. Assign the revised rubric a new commit hash.
5. Cite that hash in any later calibration report.

The forbidden move is:

> revise the rubric after looking at how the change affects TrialDiff's pass rate.

That would contaminate the reference standard and invalidate re-calibration.

## 6. Re-Certification Design

The v0.2 sample can diagnose fixes. It cannot certify fixes.

The v0.2 sample used:

- 30 of 35 available critical records;
- 30 of 51 available high records.

Therefore:

- Critical holdout is not viable. Five unused critical records cannot certify a below-20% critical false-positive rate.
- Critical re-certification requires corpus expansion.
- The 21 unused high records should be reserved as a held-out high stratum.

The v0.2.1 re-certification set must not be the same 91-record diagnostic sample.

Minimum re-certification design:

- fresh critical records from corpus expansion;
- the 21 unused high records from the 25-study alpha as a held-out high stratum;
- fresh medium and low/admin records where available;
- no reviewer exposure to revised classifier labels before priority assignment.

The buyer-facing brief remains blocked until re-certification passes on a non-tuned sample.

## 7. Candidate Rule Targets

These are candidates only. They become rule edits after the disagreement analysis and rubric-boundary review.

Likely rule edits:

1. Critical tier tightening.
   - Reserve critical for changes that materially alter primary evidence interpretation, terminal-status interpretability, or similarly high-stakes review questions.

2. `timeline_significant_shift` down-tiering.
   - Require late-stage, completion-proximate, post-completion, or independently severe magnitude/context before assigning high.

3. Results-reconciliation suppression.
   - Down-weight outcome text changes co-incident with `hasResults` transition or results-section population unless a substantive design/outcome change is visible outside results reconciliation.

4. `whyStopped` split.
   - Missing or placeholder-only stopped-trial explanation may remain critical.
   - Present but business-like or low-specificity explanation should generally be high unless other high-stakes facts are present.

Open rule question:

- whether very large timeline slips during active recruitment should trigger high priority by magnitude alone.

## 8. Stop Conditions

Do not edit classifier rules until:

- `V0.2.1_DIAGNOSTIC_PLAN.md` is committed;
- `V0.2.1_DISAGREEMENT_ANALYSIS.md` is completed;
- rubric-gap vs. judgment-variance vs. reviewer-error labels are assigned;
- any revised rubric is frozen under a new commit hash;
- re-certification design is explicitly chosen.

Do not claim v0.2.1 calibration success until:

- records are regenerated under a new `rule_set_hash`;
- a non-tuned re-certification sample is reviewed;
- the revised calibration passes the pre-registered threshold;
- the calibration report distinguishes external expert review from adversarial model review.

Do not send a buyer-facing brief until the revised calibration passes.
